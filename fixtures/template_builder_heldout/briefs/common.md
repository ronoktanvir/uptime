You are drafting contract documents for a fictional software vendor and its customers. Nothing you write will be
signed; it is a realistic drafting exercise. Invent every name: do not use any real company, person or address.

Write a service-level (availability) addendum, schedule or rider to a software-as-a-service agreement. It must state,
in the document's own words:

1. the vendor's uptime (availability) commitment, as a percentage;
2. how availability is measured, including the period over which it is measured;
3. service credits in exactly two tiers: a smaller credit when availability falls below the commitment, and a larger
   credit when it falls below a lower threshold, each as a percentage of the customer's fees;
4. a cap on the total credits for a measurement period, as a percentage of fees;
5. a deadline by which the customer must claim a credit;
6. how much advance notice the vendor gives of scheduled maintenance;
7. how a credit is paid or delivered to the customer.

Include the other ordinary provisions such a document carries (for example definitions, exclusions, how downtime is
reported, the relationship to the main agreement), so that it reads like a real document and contains other numbers
too.

Write THREE documents of the same form, labelled A, B and C, as a firm would reuse its own standard document for three
different customers. Each document uses different parties, dates and fees, and different commercial numbers
(commitment, tier threshold, credit percentages, cap, claim deadline, notice period). The wording of the form itself
stays the same apart from those details. Each document is complete and stands alone.

Answer in exactly this layout and nothing else:

===DOCUMENT A===
(the full text of document A)
===DOCUMENT B===
(the full text of document B)
===DOCUMENT C===
(the full text of document C)
===VALUES===
(one JSON object, of a structure you choose, recording for each of A, B and C the parties and every commercial value
you wrote into that document: the commitment, how and over what period it is measured, both credit tiers with their
thresholds, the cap, the claim deadline, the maintenance notice and how a credit is paid)

Use plain text for the documents (a plain-text table is fine where the style calls for one).
