# Independent evaluation: the integration works, but generalization remains blocked

The verified application completed the new 14-case first pass, but it did not onboard an unseen contract or reach a held-out dispute interpretation. Twelve actual Claude Opus 5.5 calls returned successfully; all twelve contract proposals failed validation. A separate public control completed one actual model interpretation and a signed local rebuttal receipt. That control establishes a working connected path, not unseen-vendor performance.

**Support-scope disagreement matters.** The independent author designated four contracts as supported and two as negative controls. Preserve the resulting **0/4 author-intended positive coverage**. Do not describe those four as independently confirmed instances of the frozen grammar: the corpus also introduces incident-relative filing deadlines, notice-triggered cure windows, conditional exclusions and per-incident completed-minute credits. Those obligations are broader than, or different from, the currently implemented month-end/whole-month operators. Refusing an unrepresentable obligation is appropriate; it is not automatically a parser defect. These limits describe the current implementation, not a reduction in the agreed product scope or a reason to declare the original cases invalid. Unsupported operators may be missing product functionality. The result still exposes a substantial gap between the product objective and the source language it can currently handle.

## Where the attempts stopped

The monthly-propositions validator's first error was identifier grounding for every contract. The normal public result exposed `PUBLIC_PROPOSITION_UNESTABLISHED` without a cited passage. A read-only post-terminal replay of the exact frozen provider outputs established the internal error, `Ungrounded vendor or agreement identifier.` No new model call, modified source, repaired output or repeated application attempt was used.

The resource-month alternatives also failed: four stopped at unsupported source material and two at conflicting agreement propositions. The public diagnostics provide more block-level information on that path, but the cohort does not establish that the correct contractual defect caused either intended negative to be rejected. An early identifier failure can conceal both a valid later provision and a dangerous later provision.

These findings identify two different work items. Natural contract headings, party introductions, named document titles and governing agreement identifiers need explicit, source-grounded treatment. Separately, new financial or deadline operators need deliberate support decisions and deterministic tests. Removing a grounding check or accepting a model's declaration would not resolve either problem.

Six dispute setups used the ordinary signed local candidate route and quarantined the governing source before a claim existed. The other two retained pre-frozen source-reading failures: repeated semantic roles in one interpretation and an unsupported courtesy classification in another. Those are preparation limitations, not observed errors by the held-out dispute model, because that model was never invoked.

The source-only preparation history is retained: an initial independent reading session produced eight grounding refusals; a separately recorded source-only repair produced six valid readings and two refusals. It saw neither expected outcomes nor application model results. No third preparation attempt was hidden. The current dispute harness also depends on successful governing-contract intake, which prevents this cohort from isolating interpretation quality from onboarding coverage.

## What did work independently

A separate authored public control used the normal exact-request approval, model queue, signed source review and local submission routes on the same frozen code. Its one actual Opus response produced a grounded time-bar candidate matching the separately frozen reading. The model alone left claim state unchanged. A separately scripted source projection then drafted a rebuttal, and ordinary signed local approval/submission produced a second local receipt linked to the original filing. The final state was `AWAITING_VENDOR`.

The control's stages are **$90.00 estimated, $90.00 claimed, $0.00 awarded, $0.00 posted and $0.00 applied**. Successive stages are not added together. Its two receipts are local artifacts, not Ramp objects. It used scripted evaluation roles, not an independent human's acceptance of the model candidate. The public control is excluded from all held-out denominators. Its two pre-model header preparation failures are retained separately.

Four independently authored public route probes also passed: a refused wrong-role report could not poison the genuine report; altered replay could not reprice a claim; contradictory customer evidence removed the monetary result and actions; conflicting identity produced no claim or money. The initial probe expected the wrong HTTP status for a content conflict; that assertion was corrected from 403 to the actual, appropriate 409 while preserving the financial assertions. These are public controls, not withheld tests or legal certification.
