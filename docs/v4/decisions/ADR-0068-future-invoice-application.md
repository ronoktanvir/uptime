Root read the complete reply and compared it with the source clause, existing Python/SQL guard and requested continuous narrative.

The original root64 plan applied the January USD2,400 credit to March's USD12,000 invoice, leaving USD9,600. That is not supported by the chosen chronology and existing future-invoice guard. March is issued March1 so it can be inspected before the January claim is filed March1; evidence cure and award occur March2. Replacing invoice issuance with a later balance-observation date would obscure this conflict. The existing guard requires the bill effective date no earlier than award and posted credit dates.

Choose the next regular April invoice: USD11,000 under the March amendment, issued April1 for the complete April service month. Its opening capacity date is also April1. This avoids uncertainty about the existing generic `effective_date` field for this bounded record. Receive it only when the isolated scenario event clock reaches April1; preserve March2 award/post event dates and actual wall-clock authentication/proof freshness. Apply USD2,400 once, leaving USD8,600 invoice balance and USD0 remaining credit capacity. March receives no credit allocation and its USD1,000 price finding remains unresolved. January, February and March remain the only three SLA assessments; April is a separate settlement invoice.

Opus endorsed this as the cheapest honest correction and agreed it preserves the user's goal. Root adopts the explicit date/amount/scope and non-netting controls, duplicate intake/application checks, monotonic scenario-clock checks and independent arithmetic comparison. The phrase “future invoice” can depend on a contractual reference point; this decision does not pretend to resolve that general legal ambiguity. The April record satisfies the implemented conservative guard and follows both the January service period and March award. General term inheritance, credit expiration, arbitrary bill selection and existing-unpaid-invoice remedies are not established by this fixture. The target is the single explicitly configured April bill, within the authored agreement term.

Decline the adviser's request for a new human sign-off: authoring this reversible additional regular-cycle invoice is within the user's explicit fictional-data authorization and does not change the agreed workstream priorities. The USD9,600 figure originated in root's own plan. Decline “no component reads wall-clock time”: only domain event time is controlled; authentication freshness and observed recording timestamps must retain real time. No external action or actual human approval is inferred.

Alternatives: a new source-pinned application-policy variant for existing unpaid invoices would require changes to allocation schema, SQL triggers, approval inputs and receipts. Keep that as separate future functionality rather than weakening the existing rule. Stopping at Posted is a truthful fallback if the next invoice cannot be validated, but does not complete the requested demonstration. Reordering the award before the March finding would alter the requested continuous narrative and hide the discovered composition issue; do not backdate it.
